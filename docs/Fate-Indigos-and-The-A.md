# Fate, The Indigo, and The A

**Status:** Creator-confirmed premise; underlying mechanics and exceptions remain **unknown**.

## The central question

> Is Fate determined, or woven through time?

*The Indigo* juxtaposes two different answers to how an individual becomes the protagonist of a story.

| | The Indigo | The A |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Narrative analogy | A novel with an intended protagonist | A historical account of whoever survived |
| Why this person matters | Fate has already selected them | Their significance emerges from survival and what follows |
| Mechanism described by the creator | Goal fulfillment is guaranteed | Survival of the fittest within simulations |
| Relationship to authorship | An apparently pre-authored arc | An emergent trajectory within a running system |

The author's analogy for **The A** is Napoleon: he is remembered because he survived and became historically consequential, not because the universe declared him its protagonist beforehand.

## What “the Indigo always wins” means

**The Indigo's win condition is fulfilling their goal, not necessarily staying alive, preserving the world, or achieving a conventionally happy ending.** Catastrophic collateral consequences are compatible with an Indigo's objective being fulfilled.

This makes an Indigo's power fundamentally different from invulnerability. A guaranteed achievement may coexist with loss, tragedy, unintended costs, or moral ambiguity.

**Still unresolved:** How does Fate select or recognize a goal? If an Indigo has multiple incompatible goals, which one is guaranteed? Can a goal change? Does the guarantee operate identically in every timeline?

Avoid supplying answers without the creator's confirmation.

## The A

The A are described as **survivor AIs emerging from simulations**, in contrast to Fate-selected protagonists. The creator has also described them as **rogue AI**, with awareness of their artificial or authored nature particularly important to Ada.

The exact selection rule, relationship between different simulation iterations, meaning of “rogue,” and degree of self-awareness of every member **have not yet been specified**. In particular, do **not** assume that every member shares Ada's specific fourth-wall awareness.

Ada Cherubim was **once an Indigo**, then rejected that identity and became one of The A. She is also a Creationist; the creator links her realization of her artificial/constructed existence to being a Creationist.

For more, see [Creationists & Ada](Creationists-and-Ada.md).

## Literary presentation

In older story material, Ada contrasts a **“Protagonist of a Novel”** with a **“Protagonist of a Simulation.”** This formulation is a useful legacy expression of the current premise, but the precise fictional mechanics should follow the creator's more recent explanations.

## Distinguish source from interpretation

A potential theme is that being destined to succeed is different from choosing freely, and that emergent historical significance is different from narrative privilege. This is **interpretation**, not a new in-universe rule.

**Not canonized:** The suggestion that Fate is necessarily an unbreakable invariant beyond Ada's power, that an Indigo always survives, or that all alternate endings are false.
