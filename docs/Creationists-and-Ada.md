# Creationists, Ada Cherubim & The God's Will

**Status:** Mainly **creator-confirmed**, with explicitly marked unknowns. **Major story spoilers.**

## Creationists

**Creationists** are beings tasked with creating **universes and simulations**. Their floating halos carry **plates** arranged to resemble wings. The plates represent worlds credited to their creations. The outward appearance is similar to wings, but these are **halo structures**, not conventional feathered wings.

Creationists have an instinctive aversion to Ada. Other Creationists shun her even without meeting her. **Pandora is the sole stated exception.** The underlying cause of this shared instinct has not been explained.

### Two exceptions that reveal the system's limits

| Creationist | Apparent form | Actual significance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ada Cherubim** | Twelve “wings” of halo plates | Her twelve credited sets were **stolen** by accelerating the age of universes/reality across **twelve iterations**, causing those iterations to be counted as her creations. She is regarded as the worst Creationist, with no legitimate plate achievement described. |
| **Saint Pandora / Pleiades** | Appears **zero-winged**, with **two halos** | His outer halo is composed of an **undetermined number of plates**. He alone does not share the instinct to shun Ada. |

Neither case should be simplified to “Ada was a successful twelve-world creator” or “Pandora created nothing.”

Pandora is also described as both a **Lawyer and a Ruler**; his Creationist status coexists with those roles. Do not assume the three offices are synonymous.

## Ada: identity and history

**Ada Cherubim** is:
- **Ruler of Art** — **Art as imagination, creativity, creation, and expression**, *not* “Art-magi” or a direct synonym for magical Arts.
- An **Indigo who rejected that identity** and became **one of The A**.
- A **Creationist** who became aware of her nature and the constructed reality through that condition.
- A figure associated with **chaos**, sometimes framed as its goddess.
- **Godmother of Sirius Rai**.
- A recurring antagonist in different installments of *The Indigo*, rather than the universal focus of every story.

The creator describes Ada as having attempted to **destroy the universe**, an event linked to the **creation of Alter Matter**. Its aftermath escalated into conflict involving **Overlords and Overseers**, followed by the formation of **Orders**. The exact chronology, reasons, mechanisms, and extent of the war remain to be retold.

Ada's wing/plate exploit predates the Alter Matter events; she was already the **Ruler of Art** and an **Indigo** at that stage.

## Pandora's curse

Pandora cursed Ada such that she:

> **Can speak only to the deaf; manifest only to the blind; hear only from the mute.**

This is not just an ordinary prohibition on speech. It shapes Ada's cryptic methods, who can perceive her, and how her traces surface across creative media.

Details requiring clarification: whether these conditions apply solely to in-world interlocutors, to the audience, or at multiple narrative levels; whether indirect communication may circumvent the restriction.

## Cross-media appearances: evidence rather than a conventional persona

Ada's presence is often an **easter egg**, not an ordinary on-screen character:

| Medium | Examples described by the creator |
| --- | --- |
| **Novel / book** | ASCII-like appearances, omitted/obfuscated text, unconventional textual presentation |
| **Game** | Programming-level glitches, textual interference, potentially alternate endings or route anomalies |
| **Animation** | No conventionally visible persona; hints through camera behavior, Morse code, or steganographic methods |

This is a creative framing for **meta-level interference**, not an instruction to portray every bug, odd scene, or secret as Ada. She is not meant to monopolize the narrative.

Her meta reach has been described colloquially as **Tier 0** in powerscaling. The actual limits and mechanisms **are not finalized**; do not equate that shorthand with limitless plot permission.

## The God's Will: network and masterplan

**The God's Will** is Ada's cryptic **network**. It is not described as simply rewriting reality into a wish-granting outcome. Rather, it orchestrates people, circumstances, and seemingly contradictory agendas so network participants' goals are achieved, sometimes in ways they did not want.

Participants can indirectly work **with and against one another** while advancing Ada's scheme. The creator compares this to a **Monkey's Paw by master planning**, rather than a literal reality-warping wish.

The premise is especially significant in a Prime Timeline populated by **Lawyers and Rulers**, including beings capable of reality manipulation.

### Known example: Eros, Haru, and Authority

- **Eros Rai** wanted **Haru** to become an **Authority**, with an intended route toward **Overseer of Time and Space**.
- The outcome fulfilled **the Authority objective** but not the expected office: **Ada granted Haru Overlord of Art**.
- That unexpected appointment **blocked** the planned Overseer of Time and Space outcome.

**Do not infer** from this example that Ada can always appoint any Authority freely, or how the mechanics of holding overlapping offices work. These are plot details awaiting confirmation.

## Relation to Fate

Ada's premise connects [The Indigo's guaranteed goal fulfillment](Fate-Indigos-and-The-A.md) with a deliberately engineered system that also gets people to their goals.

**Open, not canon:** Whether Ada can truly alter Fate's guaranteed result, whether her rejected Indigo status retains some protection, or whether her sabotage can only change the route and collateral damage. The creator has explicitly said the boundary that prevents Ada becoming a deus ex machina still needs designing.
